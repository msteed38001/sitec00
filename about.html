The Floppy Disk Engineer

I have been building data systems for over 30 years. I started when a floppy disk was a decent amount of space, and I've written production code in GWBASIC, QBasic, PL/B, FoxPro, VB, and now Python.

I am a data engineer. I am color-blind. I am dyslexic. I have zero patience for software that is slow, bloated, or designed by people who have never actually run a business.

I don't sell consulting hours. I don't build 50-slide PowerPoint decks. I find the data problems that are costing you money, and I build the lightweight tools to fix them.

Why I Build My Own Tools

I built my own website builder because Wix and WordPress were too slow. I built my own terminal UI because GUI frameworks choke on 75,000 rows. I built my own theme generator because I can't see a color wheel.

When the standard tools fail, I write the 500 lines of Python required to do it right.

Why I Work Remote

I don't need a massive office or an open-plan floor. I do my best work on an 8GB laptop with a 13-inch screen.

I've spent decades integrating with ERPs, building local-first web apps, and auditing supply chain data. I can do it from anywhere, and I don't need a cloud server to do it.

The "No Buzzwords" Philosophy

The modern tech industry is full of "AI experts" and "Cloud Architects" who will charge you a fortune to migrate your data to AWS.

I don't do that. I use AI like a junior programmer to write boilerplate, but I provide the 30 years of business logic. I use scripts, SFTP, and SQLite to move data because it's faster, cheaper, and more secure than a cloud pipeline.

If you want someone to configure your VPN, hire a managed service provider. If you want someone to find the $40,000 in dead inventory hiding in your General Ledger, hire me.

What is it like to work with me?

I am direct. I don't tinker. I don't build solutions for problems that don't exist.

I will tell you exactly what is wrong with your data, why it's wrong, and what it will cost to fix it. If that means telling you to write off a warehouse full of dead stock, I'll tell you that, too.

I don't do well in corporate environments that require three meetings to approve a font change. I do well when you have a specific, thorny data problem and you need it solved by next Tuesday.
